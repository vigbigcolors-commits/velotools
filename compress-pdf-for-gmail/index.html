Why Gmail keeps rejecting that attachment

Everyday Gmail accounts enforce a hard ceiling near 25 MB per message. The body, inline images, and every attachment share that budget, so a scanned lease or slide deck that looks “only a little big” still fails Send.

Phone camera exports and exported PowerPoint packs are the usual culprits: full-color pages at high DPI balloon past the limit even when page count is modest. People then paste screenshots into Drive links — workable, but not what a recipient who expects a simple attached PDF wants.

This URL exists for that exact failure: get under 25 MB while keeping the file readable in Gmail’s preview on phones and desktop.

How to fix “attachment too large” in Gmail

  1. Drop the PDF that Gmail rejected — the 25 MB email preset is already locked on this page.
  2. Tap Compress All and wait for the green size check under 25 MB before you download.
  3. Download, open Gmail, attach the new file. Still oversized? Use Split PDF for chapters, then send two messages.

Why Gmail stops at 25 MB — and what to do next

Gmail’s well-known ceiling is about 25 MB per message for everyday accounts. Workspace admins can raise it, but most people still hit the classic wall with scanned packets and slide decks.

This page is tuned for that moment: keep the file readable in Gmail’s preview, strip bulky metadata, and show a warning before you waste time on a failed Send. It is not meant for print-shop masters — keep your original if you still need perfect vector text later.

Compared with Outlook’s tighter 20 MB habit, Gmail gives a little more room, so we use a gentler email preset here. If one file is still huge after compressing, split the PDF or run a second pass on Compress PDF with the Screen preset.

People also ask about Gmail PDF size

Why does Gmail say my PDF attachment is too large?
Gmail blocks messages when the total size (body + attachments) crosses roughly 25 MB. A phone scan or slide deck often sits just over that line until you compress or split it.
How do I compress a PDF for Gmail without uploading it online?
Use this page: processing runs in your browser only. Download the smaller file, then attach it in Gmail yourself — we never see the PDF.
Will the other person still be able to open it?
Yes. You get a normal PDF that opens in Gmail preview, phones, and desktop readers. Very heavy scans become page images, so text search may be weaker.
What if it is still over 25 MB after compressing?
Split long packets into two emails, remove unused pages, or try a stronger Screen pass on the main Compress PDF tool. One full-bleed photo page is often the culprit.
Is Google Drive a better option than compressing?
Drive links avoid the attachment cap, but the recipient needs access. Compressing is better when the other person expects a simple attached file.
