iCloud Mail’s 20 MB wall on iPhone and Mac

iCloud Mail commonly refuses attachments past roughly 20 MB — in the same neighborhood as Outlook’s consumer habit, tighter than Gmail’s everyday 25 MB. iPhone camera scans of multi-page forms trip it constantly.

Mail Drop / iCloud links can bypass the attach cap, but recipients outside Apple’s ecosystem sometimes struggle. A compressed attached PDF remains the most compatible path when the other person expects a simple file.

This URL keeps color for forms and tickets while aiming under that 20 MB warning before you hit Send.

Fix iCloud Mail “attachment too large” on Apple devices

  1. Drop the PDF Mail refused — the 20 MB iCloud-oriented checker is already locked on this page.
  2. Compress All until the size status shows under 20 MB, then download to Files or your Mac disk.
  3. Attach in iCloud Mail or the Mail app. Still blocked? Split pages or use Mail Drop only if the recipient can open the link.

iCloud Mail PDF attachment FAQ

Why won’t iCloud Mail send my PDF?
Most often the attachment is over about 20 MB. Compress it here, then attach again. Mail Drop links are an alternative when both sides can open them.
Is iCloud Mail’s limit smaller than Gmail’s?
For many accounts, yes — roughly 20 MB vs Gmail’s ~25 MB. Use this page for iCloud Mail and the Gmail page when sending from Gmail.
Can I compress a PDF on iPhone without uploading it?
Yes. Open this page in Safari, compress in the browser, save the download to Files, then attach in Mail. Nothing is sent to VeloTools.
Should I use Mail Drop instead of compressing?
Mail Drop helps for huge files, but some recipients struggle with the link. Compressing is better when they expect a normal attached PDF.
Do you need my Apple ID to compress?
No. There is no Apple login. You only attach the finished PDF in Mail yourself.
