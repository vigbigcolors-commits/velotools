Outlook’s attachment ceiling vs what you think you sent

Many Outlook.com and Outlook on the web mailboxes still behave like a 20 MB attachment cap — five megabytes tighter than everyday Gmail. Desktop Outlook follows whatever Exchange admins configured, which may be the same number or a transport rule that looks like a size error.

Review threads make the problem worse: comment balloons and markup streams add weight on top of already heavy slide exports. Stripping those comments to save space can break the conversation — so this URL keeps them on purpose.

If Send fails even under 20 MB, size is not always the villain; DLP policies can still block the message after compression.

Outlook PDF attachment FAQ

Why won’t Outlook send my PDF?
Most often the attachment is over the mailbox size limit (commonly around 20 MB). Compress here, or split the file. Less often, a company rule blocks the message for other reasons.
Is Outlook’s limit smaller than Gmail’s?
For many consumer and OWA accounts, yes — roughly 20 MB vs Gmail’s ~25 MB. Use this page for Outlook and the Gmail page when you are sending from Gmail.
Do I lose comment bubbles when I compress?
Not on this Outlook URL — annotations are kept so review threads survive. Other VeloTools presets may strip them to save more space.
Can I compress without signing into Microsoft 365?
Yes. There is no Outlook login here. You only attach the finished PDF in Outlook yourself.
What if IT raised our limit to 100 MB?
You can still compress for faster mobile downloads. Ignore the 20 MB warning when you know your tenant allows more — or use the main compressor with a lighter preset.
